Definition

A defined benefit retirement plan is a plan that uses a formula to determine a participant's monthly benefit at retirement.  This is in contrast to a defined contribution plan which provides participant with a contribution amount that goes into an account which can be managed by either the participant or the plan.

Risk to sponsor
Actuarial liabilities

Who a defined benefit plan doesn't reward
Shorter service, younger individuals
Long service older individuals - the benefits tail off
People who end up dying early in retirement - no legacy account

Complexity
The complexity of a defined benefit plan formula can sometimes be a bit intimidating. A defined contribution plan is often easier to understand. But post retirement, a defined benefit plan is very easy to understand and not dependent on markets.
